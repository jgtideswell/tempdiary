Commercial Kitchen Opening & Closing Checks: Daily SFBB Guide (2026)

Updated September 2026 • 5 min read • Daily Hygiene Standards

In the Food Standards Agency’s (FSA) Safer Food, Better Business (SFBB) framework, the daily diary is structured around two non-negotiable operational bookends: Opening Checks and Closing Checks.

Visiting Environmental Health Officers (EHOs) treat these check routines as a direct barometer of your kitchen’s operational discipline. If your diary shows missing morning checks, uniform signatures signed weeks in advance, or blank closing entries, you will lose vital points under the "Confidence in Management" assessment.

Before Service Begins

Morning Opening Checks

  • Chilled Storage Temps: Verify fridges sit under 8°C (ideally 0°C–5°C) and freezers are -18°C or colder.
  • Handwashing Facilities: Sinks fully stocked with antibacterial soap, warm running water, and blue roll.
  • Prep Surfaces Sanitized: Sanitise stainless steel surfaces, chopping boards, and food contact equipment.
  • Staff Fitness to Work: Confirm team members are free of illness symptoms (48h clear rule for gastrointestinal issues).
After Service Closes

Evening Closing Checks

  • Evening Temp Sweep: Confirm chillers and freezers have recovered temperature after service and remain shut.
  • Food Covered & Labelled: No raw or prepared foods left exposed; all containers dated and covered.
  • Waste Cleared: Internal bins emptied into external refuse stores; grease traps and floors cleared.
  • Sanitisation & Shutdown: Cooking appliances isolated, extraction cleaned, and work areas wiped down.

1. The Danger of "Retrospective" Signatures

In busy operations using traditional paper SFBB binders, the most common operational failure is sign-off fatigue. Morning chefs rush into mise-en-place and forget to sign the binder; evening staff finish cleaning at 11 PM and leave the book untouched.

At the end of the week, a manager inevitably sits down with a biro and signs every single opening and closing check in one go. EHOs know this practice intimately: uniform ink flow, identical handwriting pressure, and missing variances across 14 consecutive shifts are an immediate red flag that strips away your top rating.

2. Handling "What Went Wrong" Entries

An essential part of the SFBB daily routine is documenting operational deviations. The FSA diary specifically provides space for issues encountered during opening or closing checks.

Inspectors do not penalise kitchens for noting operational issues—in fact, a diary showing occasional real-world events accompanied by immediate corrective actions is far more credible than months of "perfect" untouched pages:

  • Fridge running warm at opening: Log the temperature (e.g. 9.1°C), state that food was checked with an independent probe, and confirm that the thermostat was dialed down or engineer notified.
  • Hot water failure at handwash basin: Record the plumbing issue and note the temporary designated secondary sink used until repairs concluded.
  • Damaged seal on freezer: Document the temporary gasket repair and the order placement for new commercial seals.

3. Management Review Protocols

Under UK due-diligence rules, a senior supervisor, head chef, or business owner should verify daily logs at least once every week. Reviewing opening and closing entries confirms that staff are executing their hygiene duties in real-time, catching recurring temperature creep before it results in food spoilage or audit penalties.
