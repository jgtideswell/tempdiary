HACCP Temperature Monitoring: Critical Control Points Guide (2026)

Updated September 2026 • 6 min read • FSA SFBB Protocols

Under UK food safety regulations, every food business operator is legally required to implement and maintain a permanent food safety management system based on the principles of Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points (HACCP).

While HACCP covers many aspects of kitchen hygiene, temperature monitoring forms the backbone of controlling biological food hazards. Managing microbiological growth requires understanding exactly where your Critical Control Points (CCPs) sit, establishing unambiguous critical limits, and maintaining continuous due-diligence records.

Kitchen HACCP Temperature Critical Control Points

Process Step Biological Hazard Critical Limit (UK) Mandatory Corrective Action
1. Delivery Receipt Bacterial growth in transit Chilled ≤ 8.0°C / Frozen ≤ -15°C Reject delivery from supplier
2. Chilled Storage Listeria & pathogen multiplication ≤ 8.0°C (Best: 0°C to 5°C) Adjust dial, transfer food, engineer call
3. Cooking / Reheat Pathogen survival (Salmonella, E. coli) ≥ 75.0°C core (30s) Continue cooking until core threshold met
4. Rapid Chilling Spore germination (C. perfringens) ≤ 8.0°C within 90 minutes Divide into shallow trays, ice bath, or bin
5. Hot Holding Toxin formation in warm food ≥ 63.0°C continuous Reheat to 75°C (if <2h) or discard

1. What Makes a Step a "Critical Control Point"?

Not every single temperature measurement in a kitchen is a CCP. A process step is classified as a Critical Control Point only if:

  • A food safety hazard (microbiological contamination or proliferation) is present.
  • A specific preventive measure (such as heating to 75°C or chilling below 8°C) can eliminate or control that hazard.
  • No subsequent step in the preparation process will rectify the hazard if missed.

2. Establishing Clear Critical Limits

A Critical Limit is the strict boundary separating acceptable, safe food from potentially hazardous product.

For example, in cold holding, 8.0°C is your legal statutory limit. If a prep counter reads 8.1°C, the critical limit has formally been breached. While a kitchen might internally target 3°C as an operational target, any reading between 8°C and 63°C sits inside the hazardous "Danger Zone" where bacteria multiply rapidly.

3. The 4 Principles of Daily CCP Monitoring

An Environmental Health Officer inspecting your premises will look for verification that CCPs are monitored actively rather than purely documented on paper after the shift:

  1. Continuous vs Scheduled Frequency: Daily opening checks and evening closing checks establish the routine baseline for refrigeration and freezers. Cooking batches and hot-holding buffet line checks must occur during active service.
  2. Calibrated Instrumentation: Handheld digital probes should undergo monthly calibration checks (using an iced-water slush bath at 0°C and boiling water at 100°C) with results recorded in your calibration log.
  3. Individual Staff Attribution: Every reading must trace to a specific team member rather than anonymous ticks or single-pen handwriting across multiple weeks.
  4. Documented Non-Conformance: If a freezer drops to -12°C or a bain-marie falls to 58°C, your HACCP plan is only verified if the log records the immediate corrective resolution.
