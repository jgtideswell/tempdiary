Legal Commercial Fridge & Freezer Temperatures in the UK (2026 Guide)

Updated September 2026 • 5 min read • SFBB Standards

Whether operating an independent café, artisan bakery, dark kitchen, or busy restaurant line, commercial refrigeration temperatures are consistently one of the first physical benchmarks inspected by an Environmental Health Officer (EHO).

Failing to prove consistent cold-chain controls is one of the quickest routes from a 5-star Food Hygiene Rating down to an enforcement notice. Here is what UK food law dictates, why industry best practice differs from the statutory limit, and how to stay audit-ready every shift.

1. The Legal Maximum: 8°C

Under The Food Safety and Hygiene (England) Regulations 2013 (and equivalent devolved regulations in Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland), any food that requires chilling to prevent the growth of pathogenic microorganisms or toxins must legally be held at or below 8°C.

This statutory limit applies to all high-risk items, including dairy products, cooked meats, poultry, soft cheeses, prepared salads, and anything labeled "keep refrigerated".

2. Why Best Practice is 0°C to 5°C

While 8°C represents the legal threshold, setting refrigeration thermostats to 7°C leaves zero margin for operational error. During morning prep or peak lunch rushes, frequent door openings and hot kitchen ambient temperatures quickly drive internal temps into the Danger Zone (8°C to 63°C).

In the Danger Zone, food poisoning bacteria like Listeria monocytogenes and Salmonella multiply rapidly. Operating between 0°C and 5°C guarantees a crucial buffer that keeps core food safe even when service gets hectic.

3. Freezer Requirements: Below -18°C

Unlike chilled storage, UK food regulations do not stipulate a single universal statutory temperature figure for general catering freezers; they specify that frozen food must be stored frozen solid.

However, industry standard and Food Standards Agency (FSA) Safer Food, Better Business (SFBB) benchmark guidelines specify operating freezers at -18°C or colder. At this temperature, bacterial activity halts completely, ensuring product integrity over extended storage periods.

4. What EHO Inspectors Look For

During an unannounced inspection, Environmental Health Officers evaluate more than just the thermometer display:

  • Independent Probing: Officers frequently use calibrated temperature probes to measure the core of chilled products directly rather than relying on the unit's internal dial.
  • Opening & Closing Verification: Checking that both opening checks and closing checks are logged separately by named staff members.
  • Documented Corrective Actions: If a fridge creeps up to 9.2°C, did your log document what was done (e.g. thermostat adjusted, stock transferred, engineer booked)? An unaddressed out-of-spec reading risks immediate rating demotion.
  • Historical Records: Compliance requires businesses to produce at least six months of past temperature records upon demand.
