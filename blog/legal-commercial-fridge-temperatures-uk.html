Legal Commercial Fridge & Freezer Temperatures in the UK (2026 Guide)

Published September 2026 • 5 min read

Whether running a bustling restaurant kitchen, café, dark kitchen, or catering operation, maintaining proper commercial refrigeration temperatures is one of the first areas an Environmental Health Officer (EHO) examines during a food hygiene inspection.

Quick Reference Temperature Chart

Unit Type Legal Limit (UK) Recommended Setting
Chilled Storage / Fridges 8°C or below 1°C to 5°C
Freezers - -18°C or colder

1. The Legal Maximum: 8°C

Under The Food Safety and Hygiene (England) Regulations 2013 (and equivalent devolved regulations in Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland), high-risk food that relies on refrigeration must legally be held at or below 8°C.

High-risk items include dairy products, cooked meats, seafood, prepared salads, and anything marked "keep refrigerated".

2. Why Operating Between 1°C and 5°C is Essential

While 8°C is the legal threshold, setting your refrigeration thermostats to 7°C or 8°C leaves no safety margin. During lunch rush or delivery hours, frequent door openings can easily spike unit temperatures into the "Danger Zone" (8°C to 63°C), where bacteria such as Listeria monocytogenes and Salmonella multiply rapidly.

3. What Do EHO Inspectors Expect to See?

When an EHO arrives unannounced, they will:

  • Probe cold foods directly to check core temperatures.
  • Review consistent records proving checks occur at least twice daily (opening and closing).
  • Check that corrective actions are noted whenever a temperature deviates above 8°C.
