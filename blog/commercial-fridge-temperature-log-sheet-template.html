Commercial Fridge Temperature Log Sheet: Free Template vs Digital (2026)

Updated September 2026 • 5 min read • HACCP Systems

Every UK catering venue needs a verifiable way to demonstrate cold-chain compliance. When starting out, most head chefs or venue managers head straight to Google to download a free printable commercial fridge temperature log sheet in PDF or Excel format.

While paper templates are cheap to print, maintaining them consistently across months of busy kitchen service introduces substantial risks that can threaten your Food Hygiene Rating. Below, we break down what legal fields every log sheet must contain and examine the real operational difference between paper clipboards and digital logging.

What Every Compliant Log Sheet Must Include

If you are using a printable sheet or DIY spreadsheet, make sure it has these 6 required columns to satisfy an Environmental Health Officer (EHO):

1. Equipment Identification Specific unit names (e.g., "Walk-in Chiller", "Line Prep Fridge 1", "Pastry Freezer").
2. Date & Shift Time Exact time of check, split distinctly into morning opening and evening closing checks.
3. Recorded Temperature Exact numerical value in Celsius (°C). Target: 0°C to 5°C (legal maximum 8°C).
4. Staff Signature / Initials The specific staff member responsible for the reading to establish personal accountability.
5. Corrective Action Column Mandatory space to write the remedy whenever temperatures rise above 8.0°C.
6. Management Weekly Sign-Off Manager review signature verifying due-diligence oversight.

Why Paper Log Sheets Fail During EHO Inspections

The Food Standards Agency’s Safer Food, Better Business (SFBB) binder was introduced over two decades ago. In a modern fast-paced kitchen, paper sheets create distinct friction points:

  • Retrospective "Batch Filling": Busy staff routinely skip daily checks during hectic shifts, leading to panicked back-filling at the end of the week. EHO inspectors spot identical pen pressure and uniform handwriting instantly.
  • Zero Warning System: If a prep fridge runs at 9.8°C, a paper sheet doesn't alert anyone. Staff jot down the number without noticing it has crossed the 8°C statutory ceiling, creating documentary evidence of negligence.
  • Filing Nightmare: UK law requires caterers to produce up to six months of records on demand. Paper binders tear, get stained with grease or oil, and easily go missing from kitchen cupboards.

Summary Checklist for Kitchen Managers

Whether you remain on paper or switch to digital, ensure your food safety management plan adheres to the three golden rules of chilling compliance:

  1. Check at opening and closing: Don't rely solely on a single morning check; night-time checks ensure compressors haven't tripped before staff leave.
  2. Never ignore a reading over 8°C: Always log the corrective action (adjusting dial, moving food, servicing unit).
  3. Keep records organized: Ensure at least 6 months of historical logs are immediately retrievable when an inspector arrives.
