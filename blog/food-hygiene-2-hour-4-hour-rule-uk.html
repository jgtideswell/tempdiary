The UK 2-Hour & 4-Hour Food Safety Rule Explained (2026 Guide)

Updated September 2026 • 5 min read • Temperature Control Regulations

Managing cold holding and hot holding in commercial kitchens is relatively straightforward when items sit in a calibrated prep chiller or hot display pass. But what happens during buffet setups, afternoon tea displays, sandwich counter services, or event catering where food must sit outside strict temperature control?

Under UK food safety law, commercial operators are permitted specific statutory time exemptions known commonly as the 2-Hour and 4-Hour Rules. Misunderstanding these thresholds is one of the most common reasons catering operations receive poor Food Hygiene Ratings.

Cold Food Service

The 4-Hour Exemption

Cold food held above 8°C can only be displayed for a maximum single period of 4 hours. After 4 hours, it must be thrown away. It can never be returned to the chiller.

Hot Holding Service

The 2-Hour Exemption

Hot food held below 63°C can only be displayed for a single continuous period of 2 hours. After 2 hours, it must be rapidly chilled, reheated to 75°C, or discarded.

1. How The 4-Hour Cold Rule Operates

Under Schedule 4 of The Food Safety and Hygiene (England) Regulations 2013, high-risk foods (cooked poultry, sliced meats, soft cheeses, dairy, and filled pastries) must legally remain at 8°C or below.

The regulation permits caterers to display cold food without refrigeration for service or presentation for up to 4 hours. This window accounts for the lag phase where harmful bacteria have not yet multiplied to hazardous thresholds.

Crucial Compliance Requirement:

The 4-hour window can only be used once. You cannot display a bowl of tuna mayonnaise for 2 hours, return it to the prep fridge overnight, and place it back on display tomorrow. Once food enters the 4-hour exemption, any remnants at the end of the shift must be binned.

2. The 2-Hour Hot Holding Rule

Hot holding units (such as bain-maries, hot pie cabinets, and heated soup kettles) must legally hold food at 63°C or above.

If equipment trips, power fails, or food is presented without heating equipment (such as a buffet carve-carvery station or hot food delivery), food can drop below 63°C for a maximum window of 2 hours.

Within this 2-hour window, you must make a clear decision:

  • Reheat: Reheat the food once to a core reading of 75°C or hotter (82°C in Scotland) and place it back into compliant hot-holding.
  • Chill: Cool the food down to under 8°C within 90 minutes using a blast chiller or shallow tray routine.
  • Dispose: Safely discard the item.

3. Three Ways to Prove Compliance During an Audit

If an EHO walks into your venue during an active buffet service, they will examine whether your time control is measurable or guesswork:

  1. Maintain a Service Display Log: Document the exact start time that items left chilled storage and entered the serving counter.
  2. Batch Control: Avoid topping up existing buffet dishes with fresh stock. Fresh batches should replace depleted trays entirely so timing boundaries aren't blurred.
  3. Clear Written Corrective Logs: If a hot-holding unit drops to 59°C during service, record the deviation and confirm whether the batch was rapidly served, reheated, or disposed of.
